What Does a Good PhD Look Like?

Problem solvers, not stacks of papers

You already know how to run the tools. This class is about the part that usually goes untaught: choosing the question, and knowing why it matters.

…no technical skill is worth more than knowing how to select exciting research projects. Regrettably, this vital ability is almost never taught. — Feibelman, A PhD Is Not Enough!, p. 2

Problem-oriented, not technique-oriented

Technique-oriented“I’m the person who runs ABAQUS.” Your value is the tool. When the tool stops being the right one, so do you.
Problem-oriented“I’m working out why historic masonry fails under wind load.” The tool serves the question. You pick up new methods, or a collaborator, when the problem needs them.

Not a stack of papers

Feibelman’s first seven publications were in seven different areas of physics, each on a problem a senior scientist picked for him. Productive and employable, and still, by his own account, a habit that “seriously threatened my career.”

The PhD as a master piece

Like an apprentice’s cabinet, the dissertation “demonstrates that you have all the skills needed to be a researcher in your own right” (Rugg & Petre, p. 4). The key word is demonstrates.

Independent, not supervised

“If you expect your supervisor to know more than you about every aspect of your PhD, then you have missed the whole point” (Rugg & Petre, p. 34). By the end, you should know your problem better than anyone in the room.

What the degree certifies

  1. Identify a problem worth solving, and not yet solved
  2. Design a way to investigate it: experiments, not just runs
  3. Communicate what you found: findings, not activities
The degree is the demonstrated ability to do this cycle on your own.

Sources: Feibelman, A PhD Is Not Enough! (pp. 2, 3, 122; Ch. 6); Rugg & Petre, The Unwritten Rules of PhD Research (pp. 4, 5, 34).

What Are You Optimizing For?

Deciding what “good enough” means before the work decides for you

What does “good enough” look like at the end? Not the minimum to pass. What would you want to be able to do?

What are you optimizing for? Circle any, then rank your top two.

papersa joba skilla question you care aboutother: ____________

What are you not optimizing for? What are you willing to do less of?

Look at your last two weeks of work. Which of your answers above did it actually serve?

Check before you move on

  • Is your “good enough” something you could demonstrate, not just something you could list?
  • Does your “not optimizing for” list name something real you will give up?
  • If your work and your answers don’t match, which one needs to change?
