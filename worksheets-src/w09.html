Making Presentations

Turning your pitch deck into slides that say what you found

Bring your Oct 22 pitch deck today, printed or open on a laptop to read from. Everything you write in this packet is by hand.

Aim for a change in your audience

Every talk tries to move people from where they are to somewhere else. A From/To table names that change, once for what they think and once for what they do. “I want them to be impressed” and “I want them to understand how it works” don’t say where you want them to end up.

Two steps to a clear slide

1. Distill your message. Decide what the audience should take from the slide, and put it in the title as a sentence. 2. Align visual emphasis. Make the eye land on the evidence for that sentence.

Sentence titles

Topic titleSentence title
Model validationThe model matched the observed damage grade at 8 of 10 Mayfield walls
Mesh convergenceRefining the mesh below 50 mm changed peak stress by less than 2%
Hidden steel strip analysisHidden steel strips kept the wall standing only to 128 mph, inside the EF2 range

A sentence title says who did what, makes a claim the evidence on the slide supports, and completes “Today I learned that…”. Two lines is fine. Read only your titles in order and they should tell the story. (Numbers here are illustrative.)

Three tools to steer the eye

1. Layer the slideBackground (axes, grid, labels) in light gray, data in a quiet color, and one strong color on the marks the title is about.
2. Match form to argumentPick the chart for the comparison the title makes. Pick color for how the data relate (qualitative, accent, sequential, divergent). No rainbows.
3. Make the key check easyName what the audience has to do to verify the title. Put the things being compared next to each other, label directly, and pass the squint test.

Adapted from Ardon Shorr, “Clear thinking made visible” and “Pitching your research,” Princeton (2020). See also Michael Alley, The Craft of Scientific Presentations (2013).
