From Question to Story

A binder you build one week at a time: from what you’re working on, to a question, a hypothesis, a boundary, and a story you can tell in five minutes

Name

How this binder works

  1. Read first. Each week opens with one page on the idea, with the examples and sources from class.
  2. Worksheets. Do them in order, in writing. Each one ends with “Check before you move on”: the questions a committee would ask, asked by you first.
  3. Into your binder. Each week ends with a box. Next week’s warm-up starts by copying it. By Week 7, the boxes add up to your talk.

Three rules that run through every page

Questions, not tasks

A task has no wrong answer. A question can be wrong, and that’s what makes it research.

No tool names

The tool is the instrument. Say what you are asking it.

Learned, not did

Lead with what you found. Say what each detail means.

Sources across the binder: Booth et al., The Craft of Research; Rugg & Petre, The Unwritten Rules of PhD Research; Feibelman, A PhD Is Not Enough!; Chamberlin (1890); Judith A. Swan, “Motives in Research”; Ardon Shorr, “Telling Research Stories” (2020); Aurbach et al. (2018).
