Frameworks

The Three-Step Formula
Booth et al., The Craft of Research, 3.4.1–3.4.3
1. Topic: I am studying ___.
2. Question: ...because I want to find out ___.
3. Significance: ...in order to help my reader understand ___.

Step 1 turns a subject into a topic. Step 2 turns a topic into a question. Step 3 answers "So what?" — why anyone else should care about the answer.

Instrumental vs. Expressive Behavior
Rugg & Petre, The Unwritten Rules of PhD Research, Ch. 3

Instrumental: behavior that moves you toward your goal. Reading the six key texts in your area.
Expressive: behavior that demonstrates what sort of person you are. Reading the same irrelevant page in a state of panic.

If you can fill in all three blanks of the formula above, your work is instrumental. If you can only fill in step 1, you might be busy but not aimed.

Heilmeier's Catechism
George Heilmeier, DARPA

Before starting any project, answer:
• What are you trying to do?
• How is it done today, and what are the limits?
• What is new in your approach and why do you think it will be successful?
• Who cares? If successful, what difference will it make?
• What are the risks and payoffs?
• How much will it cost? How long will it take?
• What are the midterm and final "exams" to check for success?

Career Paths

Three Paths After the PhD (in AE)
Feibelman Ch. 6 + Rugg & Petre Ch. 16 + Becca's perspective

Tenure-line faculty: Research + grants + teaching + service. Evaluated on publications and funding. Your "reader" is the research community. Build papers, conference talks, and grant experience during the PhD.

Teaching professor: Teaching + curriculum + mentoring. Evaluated on teaching quality and student outcomes. Your "reader" is students and practitioners. Build a teaching portfolio during the PhD.

Industry: Applied R&D or practice. Evaluated on deliverables and impact. Your "reader" is clients and agencies. Build technical skills and internship experience during the PhD.

Key question: How does your step 3 "reader" change depending on which path you're on?
